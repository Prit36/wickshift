# Build WICKSHIFT

Build a complete, desktop-first browser game called **WICKSHIFT**. Act as its game designer, gameplay programmer, art director, and sound designer. Work in the project, run the game, play it, and revise what you see. Deliver a playable four-route game with a beginning, an ending, and a reason to replay each route.

Treat physical construction, working ballast, the illustrated bench, the uninterrupted launch into play, and immediate rebuilding as requirements. Tune the numeric art and animation targets by playing. If a frame is too expensive to draw, simplify background detail before sacrificing the cart's response or its legibility.

Apply the AI slop rejection criteria to the first playable route and to the finished build. A production build that compiles still needs to pass the visual, interaction, and physics checks below. Fix a failed check in the artwork, layout, or code that caused it, then repeat the same check.

Take two ideas from the references. From **Bad Piggies**, take the pleasure of making a prediction with a handful of parts, seeing what physics does to it, and immediately trying a better idea. From **Balatro**, take the attention paid to an ordinary input and the way a payoff unfolds in readable beats. Create original characters, machines, routes, artwork, interface composition, sounds, and music. Leave the pigs, crate grids, poker imagery, scoring formulas, CRT treatment, and signature effects with their respective games.

## The game

It is midnight in a seaside sorting office. The postal clerks are moths. Each sorting window has gone dark, and a lamp full of live embers is too heavy for a moth to fly. The player builds a little clockwork cart from objects on the sorting desk and drives the lamp to each window. At moth scale, an envelope seam is a curb and a brass ruler is a bridge.

**The cargo is also the control.** The heavy glass lamp slides along a brass rail on the cart. Move it toward the nose on a steep climb and watch the front wheel settle back onto the paper. A longer wheelbase makes that climb easier and leaves a long belly that can catch on the next crest. The best moment is when a player can point to one hole on the chassis and say, “Move the wheel there. I know what happened.”

### The first 30 seconds

Open straight onto the bench. A moth with one bent antenna is bracing a partly built cart with its shoulder. A free wheel is already fitted at the front. A red winding key sticks out of the drive wheel lying in the tray. Two rear sockets will accept it. Beyond the cart, the player can see the first envelope lip and a dark receiving window.

Put **“Fit the key wheel”** beside the empty mount. Picking up the wheel reveals the two sockets. Dropping it into either makes a brass click. The moth lets go, the cart settles on its wheels, and the **Dispatch** lever comes free. Pulling it opens the clamp in the same shot. Put **“Hold Space to drive”** beside the cart for keyboard play, or highlight the drive control for pointer or touch. Retire the hint after use. Aim for a first launch within 20 seconds. Let an observant player succeed on the first try.

## Construction and driving

Use a fixed, horizontal five-socket chassis with the lamp rail built in. Parts snap to visible holes on the chassis or on compatible extensions. The player chooses the wheelbase, driven axle position, ride height, suspension, and front overhang. Use the following kit.

| Part | What the player can see and change |
| --- | --- |
| Key wheel | A small driven wheel with a red butterfly key. Holding Drive spends clockwork wind and applies torque at this wheel. On loose contact it visibly spins. Moving it changes traction. |
| Cotton reel | A larger free-rolling wheel with a thread groove. Its radius clears lips that catch the small wheel. Its bulk changes the cart's stance and clearance under low paper flaps. |
| Ruler spar | A light, rigid brass extension with an extra socket at its tip. Rotate it to lengthen the wheelbase or raise an axle. A wide build is stable on a slope and easier to ground on a sharp crest. |
| Leaf fork | A curved nib-steel fork with a sprung carrier. Its mount accepts a wheel on an axle or a runner bolted to the carrier. Show the tine bending as its load travels. It adds height and can bottom out. |
| Waxed runner | An upturned piece of envelope board. Bolt it to the frame for a low, rigid skid or fit it to the leaf fork for a sprung shoe. The shoe keeps its orientation as the suspension compresses. Either mounting costs speed while the runner rubs against paper. |

- **At the bench** allow drag-to-fit and select-then-click placement. Show the actual fitted silhouette, attachment point, and rotation before committing. A hatched cross marks an invalid fit. Give its reason beside the socket, return the piece safely, and preserve inventory. Let players move, remove, rotate compatible pieces, undo and redo. A removed branch returns its children to the tray as one undoable edit. Selecting a part gives one useful sentence about what it does.
- **On the track** hold Space to drive and release to coast. Hold A/D or Left/Right to slide the lamp toward the back/front of the cart. Release to keep its position. Give it enough mass and rail travel to change axle loading visibly. It should take roughly half a second to travel end to end. Drive torque, wheel contact, spring travel, drag, and weight distribution determine the outcome. Keep terrain and physics repeatable.
- **Legal experiments** require one connected key wheel, available stock, compatible connections, and clearance between solid parts except at intended mounts. Allow foolish but legal machines to launch. A solid plumb mark below the rail shows the horizontal center of mass. When a valid socket is targeted by drag, tap selection, or keyboard focus, show a hollow mark for the proposed rig. Compute both from the actual part masses and positions, including the lamp at its starting position. A moved part contributes once. Fitting the part makes the hollow mark solid, and cancelling leaves the original mark and stock intact.
- **Scout** opens a pannable view of the actual course by click, tap, or keyboard. When the bench is idle, holding V gives a quick look at the next obstacle. Release V to restore the exact bench camera, focus, and part selection. Both views use the authored terrain and preserve the edit.
- **Delivery** occurs when the still-burning lamp reaches the receiving arch. One hard knock cracks its glass. A second distinct hard knock extinguishes it. Tune impact severity at the lamp and count a sustained collision once. A fall into a drawer ends a run. A cart that spends its last wind can still coast to a win. Offer a return to the bench when it comes to rest.
- **Retry** is one action at any moment. R or **Back to bench** restores the exact pre-run build, undo history, and full wind, ready to edit within 0.5 seconds. Also offer **Run again** after a result for players who want to change only their driving. Mark the previous decisive impact with a small removable chalk tick at its real location.

## Four jobs on one night shift

Give each job a distinct silhouette, a receiving window the player can locate while scouting, and one optional postal cancellation mark. An ordinary delivery opens the next job. Show the optional condition on the route slip before launch. Marks are earned on successful deliveries and persist independently. Aim for 15–40 seconds of driving per attempt. Start with one key wheel and one reel, counting fitted pieces. Add two spars in job two, a fork and runner in job three, and offer the full kit in the finale.

| Job | Physical puzzle | Optional mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Envelope Lip** | Start with the half-built cart. A shallow descent and one folded seam teach wheel mounting, drive, and coasting. Leave a generous runout at the window. | **Wind in hand** — arrive with at least a quarter of the spring left. |
| **Ruler Rise** | A brass ruler leans against an ink tin. A short, rear-heavy cart lifts its nose under power. On first front-wheel lift at the climb, show “Shift lamp forward” beside the lamp with the active input glyph. A longer axle spacing offers another solution. | **Short order** — deliver without fitting a ruler spar. |
| **Blotter Drop** | A sheet curls into a short drop with a long landing. The fork and runner arrive together. A sprung shoe can take the sting out of the drop, then scrub speed on the runout. A player can also coast over the lip with an ordinary wheel. Let the fork's matching mount and the shoe's visible travel teach the combination. | **Clear glass** — arrive without a crack. |
| **The Night Counter** | A ruler balances on a pencil. Its far end drops as weight passes the pivot. A long rig can get its front wheel onto the high ink roller before the ruler dips. A compact runner can ride the dip onto the lower shelf, pass under a curled flap, and rejoin the approach. Show both paths when scouting. | **Blue post** — touch the high ink roller and complete the delivery. |

On **The Night Counter**, touching the high roller leaves a single wet blue stripe on the cart part that brushed it. At the window the clerk stamps that stripe onto the job slip. The stripe shows the roller was reached. The stamp records a completed delivery. A failed attempt washes the wet stripe away on retry.

Make the route selector an illustration of the four sorting windows. On the selected route slip, print a tiny side profile traced from the actual course so the player can recognize its climb or drop. Each completed window stays lit. Give **Next window** priority after a delivery. After the fourth, pull back just far enough to see the four pools of lamplight and the moth clerks working through the night. From there, choosing any window reopens its saved build and shows its outstanding mark.

## Art direction and screen composition

**Build a playable miniature paper theatre illustrated like a vintage matchbox print.** The sorting-office pigeonholes resemble a row of sleeping houses. A thumbprint in the printing ink is almost as large as the courier. Give the moth a folded triangular coat, a postman's cap over its bent antenna, and a habit of leaning its whole weight into the clamp. These details should still read with the logo covered.

- **Color and material.** Use carbon indigo `#182638` for ink and keylines, petrol blue `#355568` and dusty teal `#79A69D` for depth, buff paper `#F3DEAE` for playable edges and labels, and vermilion `#D85D44` for the dispatch lever and key. Reserve warm honey `#FFD16C` for live fire and delivered light. Give paper cut edges, brass a hard glint at the contact face, and rubber a soft dark edge. Put ink grain and slight print misregistration on large painted surfaces. Keep type, contact edges, and attachment holes crisp.
- **Shapes and type.** Draw each part with a recognizable silhouette and one identifying interior detail. Use the same drawing for the tray, held piece, and working machine. Make a small hand-cut WICKSHIFT wordmark, use Barlow Condensed Bold for stamped labels and a readable face such as Source Sans 3 for instructions. Bundle fonts locally where available. Keep readable labels level. Give controls distinct silhouettes, such as a lever, tab, or thumbwheel, and label their actions plainly.
- **The bench at 1280 × 720.** Fill the viewport. Set the cart on a sprung clamp left of center, roughly 240–300 px wide. Let the course run from its nose toward the right-hand window. Reserve about the bottom 100 px for one shallow wooden parts drawer with recesses cut to the shape of the available objects. Print the remaining count beside each recess. Put the dispatch lever at its right end, a small route slip at top left, and pause at top right. Keep at least three quarters of the picture available to the scene. The eye should find the glass lamp first, the loose wheel second, and the lever once the wheel fits.
- **During a run.** Retract the parts drawer. Leave a compact spring-wind dial and direct driving controls near the lower edge. Keep the cart left of center on a rightward run. Lead the view by about a third of a second of forward speed, capped at 15% of the viewport width beyond its usual framing. Ease that lead and leave a vertical dead zone so each wheel bump does not bob the horizon. Frame the landing before the player leaves the lip on Blotter Drop. Foreground collidable edges get the strongest outline. Keep the wheel contact patches clear.
- **Menus and failure.** Pause folds down a routing slip with Resume, Routes, Sound, Reduced motion, and Fullscreen where supported. A failure leaves the cart and the point of contact visible with both retry actions at the edge. Show one measured observation, such as “Front runner caught the lip,” when the contacts support it. If only a fall is known, say “Lamp fell into the drawer.” Keep the delivery scene visible on success.
- **Exclude website furniture.** No landing page, top navigation bar, dashboard tiles, generic rounded inventory cards, pill badges, frosted panels, emoji parts, promotional copy, giant centered result modal, or decorative statistics. Put wear and hardware where hands or moving parts would touch. Art tools can supply original layered scenery. Code-drawn artwork also works if it has designed contours, material details, and distinct silhouettes. Draw type, interactive pieces, and collidable edges as live game elements. Replace development shapes before judging the first route.

**Drawing and hit testing.** Author each movable part with a local origin at its real attachment point, a collision outline, socket coordinates, and a generous picking shape. Use that definition to draw the drag preview and place the simulated sprite. Rotate a wheel about its hub and the dispatch handle about its hinge. Keep paper grain fixed to each surface. In a development overlay, draw fixture outlines, sockets, and pivots on the moving art. At the reference resolution the painted tyre should meet the track within about 2 CSS px of the simulated contact. Keep that overlay out of normal play.

**Art in motion.** Cache the reel's inked flanges and an off-center thread notch together. Rotate them around the hub by the measured wheel angle while the bracket stays attached to the frame. Layer the crack on the moving glass and the wet blue stripe on the part that touched the roller. Give the courier two readable climb poses. When the front support lifts on Ruler Rise while the rear still bears weight, the moth reaches toward the lamp. Once the nose settles it grips the rail again. Debounce brief contact chatter. With reduced motion, switch directly between the held poses. The moth communicates the condition through its silhouette and has no effect on the cart's mass.

**Type and spacing.** Give the interface a small set of text sizes and shared edge insets. At desktop reference size start around 16–18 CSS px for instructions and 14 px for compact counts. Use steady-width numerals for the changing wind readout. Keep one outline at each adjoining edge. Put hints clear of the selected socket and the next landing. Give thumb controls a 44 × 44 CSS px target or larger. At compact sizes collapse secondary actions into the routing slip before shrinking primary controls. Test long labels and the fallback font for clipping.

## What an input should feel like

Use these timing ranges as tuning targets. Drive feedback from the event that actually happened. The player can interrupt a transition or skip a result. Bind inputs immediately and keep the moving part under the pointer.

| Event | Picture and timing | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pick up and fit | Lift the part 2–3 px away from its shadow. On a valid snap the gap closes, the part overshoots by a pixel or two, and it settles in 90–140 ms. A rejected fit briefly hatches just the incompatible hole. | A dry material tap on pickup, a short two-piece clink on attachment, a low wooden knock on rejection. |
| Dispatch | The lever moves on press. Clamp jaws spring apart and the shelf folds down over about 220 ms. Preserve the machine's screen position through release. Accept held throttle as the jaws open. | A low latch clack, then the ticking key takes over. |
| Shift and land | The lamp rides the rail continuously. The flame leans with acceleration. A tiny glint at the brass stop confirms full travel. A fitted fork flexes on landing. A hard impact opens a visible crack on the glass. | A short slide and end-stop tick. Give the sliding shoe a soft filtered-paper hiss scaled by contact speed that stops when it lifts. Use two short, decaying metallic tones for a brass strike and a separate glass ping for a crack. |
| Lose the flame | The wick contracts to an orange dot and the moth catches its hat. Keep the failed pose readable and the retry button immediately live. Back to bench closes the clamp around the saved build. | A soft puff followed by the quiet of the stopped mechanism. |
| Deliver | Let the ember catch in the receiving wick. Roughly 150 ms later the window lights and the clerk's silhouette appears. Then the job slip catches its ink stamp. Complete the sequence in under a second, with the next action already available. | A wick hiss, one tuned desk bell, then the flat thump of a rubber stamp. Add one answering note for an earned optional mark. |

Give the room a quiet bed of paper rustle and distant timber ticks. Compose an original four-note phrase from the sound of a plucked ruler and a felted desk bell. Let several bars of silence separate phrases so the player can hear what the cart is doing. Start audio after the first user gesture. Provide separate effects and music levels and a mute control. Make a muted run fully readable. Reduced motion removes camera shake, decorative parallax, and animated transitions while preserving visible physical movement, the crack state, and immediate feedback.

## Implementation and usability

### Runtime and physical behavior

For a new project, use TypeScript with strict checking, Vite, Canvas 2D, Planck.js, and Web Audio. Reuse an existing capable game stack if the project already has one. A static build must run when served from a normal web server. Bundle the artwork, fonts, and sound definitions with it. The player can complete a run with third-party requests blocked. Keep runtime AI services, analytics SDKs, and a backend out of this single-player game.

- **One clock.** Drive presentation with one `requestAnimationFrame` loop. Accumulate elapsed time and advance physics in fixed 1/60 s steps. Interpolate previous and current transforms for rendering. Cap catch-up work at five steps per frame and discard excess backlog. On focus loss or tab hide, pause and clear held input. Reset the accumulator on resume. Wind, damage, and success use simulation ticks. A 120 Hz monitor must not make the cart faster.
- **One physical model.** Use metres and radians inside the simulation and a single world-to-screen conversion. Use rigid fixtures for the frame, axle joints for the wheels, suspension for the leaf fork, and a limited motorized prismatic joint for the lamp. Apply torque at the driven hub and let friction move the cart. An airborne drive wheel may spin and twist the chassis while the cart's center of mass follows its flight. Exclude scripted hill assists, invisible win forces, and automatic self-righting. Generate the visible terrain edge and its collider from the same authored profile.
- **Dispatch and contact handling.** Seat the supports on the start platform without penetration and release the clamp without adding an impulse. Filter intended overlaps such as axle and fork. Preserve contact with terrain and sensors where the rules require it. During a physics step, copy fixture IDs and any available contact positions and impulses. Resolve queued events after the step, including any body or joint removal. Discard pending work from an abandoned run. Join consecutive terrain segments cleanly so only an authored lip can catch the runner.
- **Impact and rewards.** Distinguish sustained scraping from a fresh landing. Group contacts from one landing and evaluate the load transmitted to the lamp once, using its change in velocity along the impact direction or a calibrated contact impulse. A wheel touching a ramp each tick must never count as a string of new cracks. Handle the receiving arch with a sensor, resolve the lamp condition for that tick, and grant delivery once if it is still lit. Store progress before starting the stamp animation.
- **State ownership.** The workshop owns the editable blueprint and undo history. Each placement records its part, parent socket, and orientation. Derive available stock from the kit and placements. Validate the whole edit before committing. Dispatch creates a fresh simulation from a snapshot. Build, Scout, Run, Result, and Routes each accept their own actions. Pause retains its underlying mode. On retry dispose of run-owned bodies, listeners, timed effects, and voices. A double press or a late callback must not create a second cart or reward.
- **Code that can be checked.** Put part measurements, socket rules, and route definitions in typed data shared by the bench and simulation. Keep physics and save mutations out of draw functions. If a DOM framework is already present, keep per-frame wheel transforms out of its component state. Make fixtures exercise the same actions the player uses. Remove unused starter components, abandoned implementations, gameplay TODOs, and success paths that exist only in tests.

### Controls, sound and recovery

Support mouse and keyboard throughout. Tab moves focus, arrows choose a part or socket, Enter activates the focused control, Q/E rotate a compatible selected part, Delete removes it, and Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes. Give redo a visible control and a conventional shortcut. Esc pauses, with focus returned to the prior control on resume. For mouse-only driving, click the drive lever to latch it on or off, leaving the pointer free to work the lamp. Show the lever's position clearly. On touch, place the two lamp directions under the left thumb and a hold-to-drive pad under the right, and support simultaneous presses. Reset latched and held inputs on retry. Use visible focus, generous hit areas, and shapes as well as colors for valid and invalid states.

- **Input coordinates.** Use Pointer Events. Capture the pointer for each drag or held control and track its ID. Convert client coordinates to canvas-local CSS pixels, subtract any viewport offset, then apply the inverse camera transform for world hits. Scale the backing buffer for device pixel ratio exactly once. Screen-space controls get first chance to handle input. Commit a valid `pointerup` once. On `pointercancel`, lost capture, or blur, abandon any still-pending drag without changing stock and clear the held action. Restrict touch gesture suppression to the game surfaces that need it.
- **Control states.** Implement idle, focused or hovered, pressed or held, and unavailable states for each main control. A dragging part also needs legal, illegal, committed, and cancelled states. Keyboard, pointer, and touch dispatch the same underlying actions. Expose named, focusable controls for the painted UI. When a drawer retracts, retire its tab stops and move focus to a valid control. Keep the pause slip keyboard-operable and announce a delivery or loss once. Honor the system reduced-motion preference on first launch and remember the player's override.
- **Visual and audio timing.** Emit one event per committed fit, rejection, hard impact, or delivery. Use that event's position and intensity to drive the local animation and sound. Put effects and music on separate gain controls. Repeated wheel contacts should not stack glass pings. Cancel pending run sounds on retry and suspend loops when the tab is hidden. A missing sound asset or a browser awaiting an audio gesture leaves physics and input responsive.
- **Save and recover.** Version the local save. Store route progress, marks, settings, and per-route blueprints. Save after meaningful edits and results, away from the hot frame loop. Validate IDs, sockets, counts, and orientation on load. If one route's build is corrupt, restore that route's starter rig and preserve its earned mark and the other jobs. With storage disabled or full, play the session in memory and show one short message about saving.

Target smooth 60 fps on a Windows laptop with integrated graphics. Fit a browser window at 1280 × 720, 1366 × 768, and 1920 × 1080. Also exercise an 844 × 390 landscape viewport. Fit the scene uniformly, anchor controls inside the visible bounds, and retest coordinate mapping after resize, zoom, and fullscreen. Cache static illustration and material textures. Draw moving objects and changed HUD values from current state. Begin with a render pixel-ratio cap of 2 and lower background detail first if profiling shows a bottleneck.

## Build order and evidence of completion

1. Finish **Envelope Lip** end to end first, with final-style cart, moth, bench, one illustrated course, input feedback, sound, failure, and delivery. Run it in a browser. Inspect the bench at normal size and as a thumbnail. If the loose wheel looks like an icon in a menu or the playable edge disappears into background art, fix the drawing and hierarchy before adding routes.
2. Implement the other three jobs. Tune using the same physics and controller used by players. Keep a reproducible winning blueprint and input sequence for each job and each optional mark. On Ruler Rise, pick one short build and repeat the same throttle input. With the lamp at the rear its front wheel should lift and it should stall or tip. With the lamp shifted forward it should crest the ruler. Tune mass, axle position, and slope until this happens through physics. On The Night Counter, complete both paths with genuinely different builds.
3. Play the whole shift through the normal UI. Include a removed branch plus undo, a cracked-lamp finish, a failed landing, an optional mark, route unlocks after refresh, mute, and reduced motion. If a first-time player is available, see how long their first launch takes and ask what single change they would make after a failed run. Record what they actually did. An automated walkthrough measures control behavior and completion.
4. Deliver the implemented project, exact local run/build commands, and a concise verification report. Give each check a result, the browser and viewport, and its evidence or exact reproduction steps. Identify emulated devices and label unavailable hardware, browser, or human playtests as unverified. Keep development fixtures out of the player interface. In a chat-only coding environment, supply the complete runnable files and flag checks that still need execution.
